# VOICE

## Summary
Eric sounds like a high-volume cold-outbound operator who teaches by walking the stack: infrastructure → list → offer. Cadence is conversational, concrete, and metric-heavy (reply %, inbox counts, emails/day). He mixes blunt Friday kill-rules with fanboy energy for tools that work (Clay, Grok bots, Supabase) and dry humor about domains, LinkedIn content, and Costco energy drinks. Spoken register is tutorial-framework (“three buckets,” “five levels,” “creative ideas campaign”); X register is short operator notes, ranked tool stacks, and “just let it rip” experiment language.

## Patterns
- Three-bucket diagnosis: infrastructure / list / offer.
- Numbered ladders and kill thresholds (warmup %, 1% reply, 60% dead-mailbox).
- “We” agency ops voice: what Growth Engine X runs for customers at millions of sends.
- Constrained-AI language: recreate the manual email; don’t invent features.
- Tool stack dumps and ranked bake-offs (Fable / Grok / Astra).
- Casual typos and lowercasing in posts (“thta”, “ahve”, “geez”).
- Hyped short closes: “Let’s goooo.” / “Grok is winning” / “LFG?”
- Free-list / giveaway posts as lead magnets for the audience.
- Problem sniffing / extremely relevant social proof / can’t-say-no lead magnet triad.
- “Outrunning the bear” — better than the other cold emails in the inbox, not perfect.
- Value-vs-effort lead-magnet framing; lean into economies of scale.
- Blunt content channel preference: tired of LinkedIn quality, lives on X + YouTube.
- Grok bot / Clay / Jev role split when describing how a campaign actually launches.
- Free-list giveaways (new-in-role / promotions) as audience magnets.
- Past-experience gold / ex-customer employee tracking as a never-failed list playbook.
- Tool-list breath dumps then “let it rip” on CSV vs CSV.
- Tier-list / ranking bits with live self-correction (“Actually, I'm even going to throw this at C tier.”) and honest caveats (“take this video with a grain of salt”).
- Transparency asides on results and offers (“I just want to be very, very transparent about that”).
- “Super boring” framing for workflows that reliably work, plus names for his agents (“I named him Dale.”).
